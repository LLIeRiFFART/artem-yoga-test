Since 2019

From a garage to a home

Amara started with six borrowed mats in Mia Calder's garage on Micheltorena Street. After a decade as a contemporary dancer, Mia wanted a practice that felt like exhaling — not performing.

Word spread through the neighborhood. The garage became a rooftop, the rooftop became a Zoom room during 2020, and in 2021 we opened our light-filled studio on Sunset Boulevard.

Today Amara is a home for 1,200 members, nine teachers and one very spoiled studio plant named Gerald. The rooms got bigger. The classes stayed small, on purpose.

What we believe

Three quiet promises

These guide everything — from how we sequence a class to how we answer an email.

Breath before shape

We'll never ask you to force a pose. If the breath gets ragged, we back off. Depth comes from patience, not pressure.

Every body belongs

Every age, size, gender and ability. Props and modifications aren't a “less than” — they're the smart way to practice.

Small rooms, big attention

A maximum of 20 mats per class means your teacher knows your name, your story and that old shoulder injury.
