Your first yoga class: what nobody tells you

Mia Calder · Founder September 2

Every week, someone walks into Amara, stands at the door for a second and whispers: “I've never done this before.” Then they look around the room as if everyone else was born in lotus pose. They weren't. Here's what I wish someone had told me before my first class in 2009.

You don't need to be flexible

Saying you're not flexible enough for yoga is like saying you're too dirty to take a shower. Flexibility is a side effect of practice, not a prerequisite. Most of our strongest students couldn't touch their knees on day one.

“The pose is not the goal. The pose is the place where you practice paying attention.”— Mia

What actually happens in a class

A typical 60-minute Amara class follows a gentle arc. Knowing it in advance takes away half the nerves:

  1. Arriving (5 min). Sit or lie down, notice your breath. Nobody is watching you — they're all busy noticing theirs.
  2. Warming up (10 min). Cat-cow, gentle twists, a few slow sun salutations.
  3. Standing flow (25 min). Lunges, warriors, balances. Take child's pose whenever you want — it's always allowed.
  4. Cooling down (10 min). Hip openers and stretches on the floor.
  5. Savasana (10 min). Lying still with your eyes closed. Yes, it's part of the class. Yes, it might be the hardest part.

What to wear and bring

Something you can move and breathe in. Leggings or shorts, a fitted top that won't fall over your face in downward dog. Practice barefoot. Bring water — we provide mats, props, towels and the tea afterwards.

The unwritten etiquette

Arrive 10 minutes early, silence your phone, and tell the teacher about any injuries or pregnancy. If you need to leave early, set up near the door and skip savasana quietly. That's honestly it. There's no secret handshake.

And if you feel awkward?

You will, a little. Everyone does. Wobbling is not failing — it's your nervous system learning something new. Come back three times before you decide how you feel about yoga. By the third class, the room will feel like yours.
